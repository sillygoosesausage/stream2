## Headline: lead features (Task 2.4 spillover)

The largest finding of the project so far, and it came out of the cross-station lead/lag check.

For a row at hour *t*, the target is PM2.5 at *t+1*. The test set is contiguous, so **the row at
*t+1* is also in the test file**, carrying observed PM10/CO/NO2/SO2/O3/weather for the very hour
being predicted. 99.25% of test rows have their *t+1* row present.

The task is therefore a **nowcast**, not a forecast: estimate PM2.5 at *t+1* from every other
measurement taken at *t+1*.

| Features | Fold A | Fold B |
|---|---|---|
| raw baseline | 28.97 | 32.86 |
| + lead-1 covariates | 23.11 | 24.95 |
| + city-wide aggregates + leads | 23.27 | **23.71** |

**−9.15 RMSE on fold B.** This already beats the illegal `pm25_now` model (25.18) and nearly
reaches illegal persistence (22.25). Written up as Fact 4 in PLAN.md.

**Consequence for Phase 7:** the recursive experiment's headroom has largely been taken by legal
means. Its priority drops from "biggest upside" to "optional".

## Task 2.1 — Missingness structure

- Present-row null rates are mild: CO worst in train (4.4%), O3 worst in test (2.0%).
- **Absent hours** (no record at all) are 2.08% of the train grid and 2.04% of test — reassuringly
  similar, so the test set is not systematically gappier.
- **Gaps are bimodal.** Median gap is 1–2h, but the tail is long: PM10 max 344h, CO max **1517h**
  (≈63 days). Roughly 46–65% of all null hours sit in gaps longer than 6h.
- **Stations fail semi-independently** — mean pairwise correlation of the missingness indicator is
  0.23 (PM10), 0.25 (CO), 0.46 (TEMP).
- Missingness carries mild signal: mean target is 94.4 when O3 is null vs 77.6 when present;
  weather nulls associate with *cleaner* hours (~65 vs 78).

**Implications:**
1. A single imputation strategy is wrong. Short gaps (≤6h) are safe to interpolate; multi-week
   outages are not — they should stay NaN.
2. Because stations fail independently, **cross-station imputation is available**: when one
   station's PM10 is missing, the other 11 usually have it. This is a better fill than either
   interpolation or NaN.
3. Missingness-indicator columns are worth testing (D7), but the effect looks small.

## Task 2.7 — Artefacts

- **The 999 ceiling is a non-issue.** Exactly 1 row at 999 and 3 rows ≥900. Not censored data.
  The `[2, 999]` post-processing clip is harmless but will almost never bind.
- The target is **integer-valued** (min 2). Rounding predictions would not help RMSE — don't.
- **Frozen sensors are real**: longest run of an identical value is SO2 **204h**, CO 77h,
  PM2.5 41h, PM10 25h. Worth a "value unchanged for N hours" flag.
- No negative pollutant readings.
- CO is coarse: only 131 distinct values, integer, range 100–10000.

## Task 2.2 — Target distribution and error concentration

Quantiles: p10 = 10, median 55, p90 = 179, p99 = 354, p99.9 = 551, max 999. Skew 2.01;
log1p skew −0.33 (log1p nearly symmetrises it).

**Where the variance lives — this sizes Phase 8:**

| Worst hours | Threshold | Share of total variance |
|---|---|---|
| top 1% | y ≥ 354 | 22.4% |
| top 5% | y ≥ 235 | **49.9%** |
| top 10% | y ≥ 179 | 63.3% |
| top 25% | y ≥ 108 | 73.0% |

**Half of all squared error comes from 5% of hours.** The Phase 1 baseline confirms it: fold B
RMSE runs 10.8 on the cleanest decile to 87.2 on the worst. Phase 8 is justified.

That log1p skew of −0.33 also confirms the prediction in Phase 8: a log-target model optimises the
wrong thing for RMSE, but will be a well-decorrelated ensemble member.

## Task 2.3 — Temporal structure

Autocorrelation:

| lag | PM2.5 (illegal) | PM10 (legal) | target vs lagged PM10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1h | 0.968 | 0.931 | 0.809 |
| 3h | 0.881 | 0.804 | 0.729 |
| 6h | 0.764 | 0.661 | 0.623 |
| 12h | 0.592 | 0.490 | 0.480 |
| 24h | 0.398 | 0.347 | 0.326 |
| 72h | 0.047 | — | — |

PM10 tracks PM2.5 closely enough to be a genuine substitute for the banned history. Signal is
largely exhausted by ~24h, so lags beyond 24h are low priority.

- **Hour of day:** bimodal, peak 20:00–00:00 (~86), trough 15:00–16:00 (~72).
- **Day of week:** Fri/Sat highest (82–83), Mon/Wed lowest (~74). Weak but real.
- **Year-over-year, season-matched (Sep–Feb):** mean 93.5 (2013) → 86.8 (2014) → 83.2 (2015).
  Medians fall much faster: 64 → 60 → 42. Air is improving, and the improvement is concentrated in
  ordinary hours rather than in the extremes.

## Task 2.4 — Cross-station structure

- Same-hour PM2.5 correlation across the 12 stations: **mean 0.88**, range 0.79–0.97.
- A station's PM2.5 is explained by the mean of the *other 11* with **mean R² = 0.878**.
  Central sites (Guanyuan 0.948, Aotizhongxin 0.936) are most predictable; outlying northern
  sites (Dingling 0.765, Changping 0.789, Huairou 0.800) least.
- City-wide PM10 correlates 0.833 with a station's next-hour PM2.5 — nearly matching the station's
  *own* PM10 (0.847), and carrying partly independent information.

**Pollution here is regional, not local.** Tier D is confirmed as a high-value tier, and this is
what surfaced the lead-feature finding above.

## Task 2.5 — Meteorology

- **Wind speed is powerfully predictive**, monotonically: mean target 99.2 at WSPM ≤0.5 falling to
  21.8 at WSPM >6.
- **Direction × speed interaction is dramatic** and matches Beijing's known geography — clean air
  off the north-western mountains, dirty air off the southern industrial plain:

| | calm (≤2) | windy (>2) |
|---|---|---|
| NNW / NW / N | 71–83 | **20–23** |
| ESE / E / SE | 100–104 | 84–90 |

  Strong wind only cleans the air *from the right direction*. A plain one-hot on `wd` cannot express
  this — the u/v vector encoding in Tier B is required.
- **Derived humidity beats its raw inputs by a wide margin:**
  - relative humidity (Magnus, from TEMP+DEWP): corr **0.374**
  - dew point depression (TEMP−DEWP): corr **−0.389**
  - versus raw DEWP 0.124 and TEMP −0.122

  Mean target climbs 24.4 → 108.5 across RH deciles. These two derived features are essentially
  free and should be in the first feature tier built.
- Rain: only 4.06% of rows; mean target 58.9 raining vs 78.9 dry.
- **Pressure tendency is a dud** (3h change, corr −0.041). Deprioritise it.

## Task 2.6 — Train vs test drift

Season-matched comparison (train Sep–Feb vs test) — the honest one, since test is Sep–Feb:

| | train Sep–Feb | test | Δ% |
|---|---|---|---|
| PM10 | 107.6 | 112.5 | +4.6% |
| NO2 | 57.5 | 59.2 | +2.9% |
| CO | 1514 | 1553 | +2.6% |
| O3 | 32.2 | 31.9 | −0.9% |
| **SO2** | **21.7** | **12.1** | **−44.4%** |

*(Ignore the all-rows table's TEMP/DEWP percentages — those divide by a near-zero mean and are
meaningless. Use absolute differences for those columns.)*

**SO2 has collapsed by 44%** while every other pollutant is roughly stable. This is consistent with
Beijing's coal-to-gas conversion taking effect, and it is the single biggest modelling hazard found:
SO2 correlates 0.499 with the target, so the model will learn an SO2→PM2.5 relationship calibrated
to 2013–2015 levels that no longer holds.

**Adversarial validation:** a classifier separates train Sep–Feb from test with **AUC 0.9475** —
substantial drift. Top discriminators by gain: SO2 (14%), DEWP (14%), TEMP (11%), PRES (11%),
O3 (10%). Some of this is unavoidable year-to-year weather variation, but SO2 leading the list is
the concerning part.

Against fold B's validation window specifically (Sep 2015–Feb 2016), the test period is *dirtier*:
PM10 +16.9%, NO2 +13.2%, CO +4.3%. So a model tuned on fold B is more likely to **under**-predict
on test than over-predict — the opposite of the direction assumed when Phase 10 was written.

Station balance is even across train and test (~4,150–4,300 test rows each). Wind direction shifts
somewhat (NE 10.6%→17.2%, W 5.2%→2.2%).
